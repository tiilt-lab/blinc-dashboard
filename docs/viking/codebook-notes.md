## The case
- Pat: real estate developer and landlord. Sandy: owner of a carpentry business. Dispute over work done at a condo project.
- Sandy must pay a $1.4M bill for oak within 15 days or go bankrupt; owes Pat a $200K loan at 10 %; rents space from Pat ($10K/month); may have to sell the house.
- Pat wants to invest in a lakefront property and a hotel and must act immediately; views the amended bill as fair.
- The contract was amended by **Fawn** (the email examples say "Alex"; both names may appear) "based on past practice"; "changes must be in writing".
- If Sandy goes bankrupt the trustee seizes assets; Pat recovers less than the $210K loan and cannot offset it.
- One BATNA-superior outcome: rental space agreed, Pat pays at least $1.4M, Sandy sells house and repays loan, Pat gives Sandy future work.

## Interests / Rights / Power (her definitions, slides 25–31)
- **Power**: ability to coerce and harm the other side's interests — threats, force, strike, status; raised voices, insults, table pounding.
  Viking: Pat — the contract, forcing Sandy's bankruptcy, future work for Sandy's team; Sandy — declaring bankruptcy.
- **Rights**: independent standards with perceived legitimacy or fairness — contracts, legal rights, precedent, norms.
  Viking: Pat — the contract, "changes must be in writing"; Sandy — Fawn's authority to amend based on past practice, "acted in Pat's best interest".
- **Interests**: needs, desires, concerns, motivations, priorities — WHY do you want what you want; WHAT do these interests allow; HOW can we work toward solutions.
  Viking: Pat — invest in lakefront property/hotel, keep team together; Sandy — avoid bankruptcy, keep house.
- Recommended opening: briefly mention rights → transition to interests → power only when repeated attempts at interests fail.
  Debrief question: "How did you open the negotiation? Interests, rights, power?" → report each team's opening move and RIP transitions.
- "Wise threat" (last resort): "If you do not ____, then I will ____." — code as power.

## Escalating (her "instructions to be angry", slide 17)
Some participants are TOLD to display anger for at least the first 10 minutes. Communication irritators to code as escalating:
telling the other side what to do; labelling their behaviour negatively ("unfair", "unreasonable"); accusations of intentional
violations; blaming; large demands; dismissing arguments without a counterpoint; interrupting; raised intensity.
Anger obstacle (slide 8): focused on the past, vindictive → also past/blame framing.

## Defusing (slide 39 "Defuse their anger")
Keep your cool and take a break; seek to understand why they are angry; give voice to their anger — not "Calm down" but
"I can see that you're angry, and I'm sorry we're in this situation. Tell me what's on your mind."; help them focus on
underlying interests — "What do you want going forward?" "What would help to put this behind us?"

## Interest-oriented listening (slides 40–43)
Don't interrupt; be curious; ask questions that help you understand ("Tell me more", "Help me understand");
paraphrase what you heard (not what they said); sum up what you think their interests are; then offer solutions.
Weak listening: internal dialogue of commenting, judging, insulting.

## Summary items worth reporting per team (beyond the four dimensions)
- Opening move: dominant RIP code in each team's first ~3 minutes, and the sequence of RIP transitions.
- Irritator count and the time of the last escalating utterance (did the angry side de-escalate after ~10 min?).
- First interest-focused question by the non-angry side (the move the instructions reward).
